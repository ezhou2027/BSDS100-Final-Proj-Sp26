# BSDS100-Final-Proj-Sp26

*Overview*
Collaborated with three others to put together a presentation as part of my introduction to data science class in May 2026.
Our overarching theme was how GPA plays a role in everyday life - from family history to depression rates. My focus was on the correlation between average major GPA and dropout rates. 


# Findings
<img width="785" height="425" alt="Screenshot 2026-07-24 at 20 08 22" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e94b01d2-f61f-42ab-845d-9ca3f005b538" />
Taking most of my data from UC Berkeley's public repository, I find that the average GPAs of the worst performing major, astrophysics majors, were lower than the average GPAs of the best performing majors, art majors. This is consistent from the 2015-16 school year to the the 2024-25 school year.
My conclusion here was that art majors perform better than science majors. 

<img width="811" height="415" alt="Screenshot 2026-07-24 at 20 13 07" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e8a0e09a-de06-41e5-b376-42b2a510c222" />
Here, the GPAs of the arts majors are more varied, while the science majors are more consistent. This can likely mean different teaching styles across the humanities, whereas the sciences + math classes are consistentally taught. 

Now, onto UC Irvine. 
UC Irvine's most dropped major, shockingly, was sociology, and its least dropped major was psychology. Since sociology was part of the humanities spectrum, this disproved my earlier conclusion.

<img width="778" height="431" alt="Screenshot 2026-07-24 at 20 15 23" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b9772128-f535-4e20-bedf-e39200752c1c" />

Unsurprisingly, sociology majors' average GPAs are worse than their psychology counterparts. In recent years, the sociology and film/media studies majors have been in decline, while majors like engineering and the sciences have kept a relatively low drop rate. 

This might be due to the changing job market and the perception that "the humanities are useless in the industry". But this is not indicative of dropout rates, as physics and math still hold a high dropout rate due to its difficulty. 

# Conclusion
Average GPA is not a definite indication of dropout rates.
